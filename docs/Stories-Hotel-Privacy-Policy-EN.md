A second one is handled the same way. "Adatvédelmi felelős" is the person responsible for data protection; the GDPR's Data Protection Officer is "adatvédelmi tisztviselő" in Hungarian. The original does not use that term, so neither does this translation — see §1.1.

## 2. Basic concepts of data protection

**2.1. Personal data.** Any data that can be associated with a specific
(identified or identifiable) natural person, and any inference that can be
drawn from the data about the data subject. Personal data retains this quality
throughout processing for as long as its connection with the data subject can
be restored. A person shall be regarded as identifiable in particular where
they can be identified, directly or indirectly, by name, an identifying mark,
or one or more factors characteristic of their physical, physiological, mental,
economic, cultural or social identity.

**2.2. Consent.** The voluntary and definite expression of the data subject's
wish, based on adequate information, by which they give unambiguous agreement
to the processing of personal data concerning them, whether in full or as
regards particular operations.

**2.3. Objection.** A declaration by the data subject objecting to the
processing of their personal data and requesting that the processing cease or
that the processed data be erased.

**2.4. Data controller.** The natural or legal person, or organisation without
legal personality, who or which determines the purpose of the processing of
personal data, takes and implements the decisions concerning the processing
(including the means used), or has them implemented by a processor it has
engaged.

**2.5. Processing.** Irrespective of the procedure applied, any operation or
set of operations performed on personal data, such as collection, recording,
registration, organisation, storage, alteration, use, transmission,
disclosure, alignment or combination, blocking, erasure and destruction, as
well as the prevention of further use of the data. Taking a photograph or
making a sound or image recording also constitutes processing, as does the
recording of physical characteristics capable of identifying a person (for
example a fingerprint or palm print, a DNA sample, or an iris image).

**2.6. Transmission of data.** Where the data is made accessible to a
specified third party.

**2.7. Disclosure.** Where the data is made accessible to anyone.

**2.8. Erasure of data.** Rendering the data unrecognisable in such a way that
it can no longer be restored.

**2.9. Blocking of data.** Rendering it impossible, permanently or for a
specified period, to transmit, access, disclose, transform, alter, destroy,
erase, combine or align the data, and to use it.

**2.10. Destruction of data.** The complete physical destruction of the data
or of the medium containing it.

**2.11. Processing on the controller's behalf.** The performance of technical
tasks connected with processing operations, irrespective of the method and
means applied to carry out the operations and of the place of application.

**2.12. Processor.** The natural or legal person, or organisation without legal
personality, who or which processes personal data on the controller's
instructions — including instructions given under a provision of law.

**2.13. Third party.** A natural or legal person, or organisation without
legal personality, who or which is not the data subject, the controller or the
processor.

**2.14. EEA State.** A Member State of the European Union and any other State
party to the Agreement on the European Economic Area, and further any State
whose nationals enjoy the same status as nationals of a State party to the
Agreement on the European Economic Area under an international treaty
concluded between the European Community and its Member States and a State not
party to that Agreement.

**2.15. Third country.** Any State that is not an EEA State.

## 3. Principles of data protection

Personal data:

a) shall be processed lawfully and fairly and in a manner transparent to the
   data subject ("lawfulness, fairness and transparency");
b) shall be collected only for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and
   not processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes; in accordance
   with Article 89(1) GDPR, further processing for archiving purposes in the
   public interest, for scientific and historical research purposes or for
   statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the original
   purpose ("purpose limitation");
c) shall be adequate and relevant to the purposes of the processing and
   limited to what is necessary ("**data minimisation**");
d) shall be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable
   step must be taken to ensure that personal data which is inaccurate for the
   purposes of the processing is erased or rectified without delay
   ("accuracy");
e) shall be stored in a form which permits identification of data subjects for
   no longer than is necessary for the purposes of the processing; personal
   data may be stored for a longer period only where it is processed for
   archiving purposes in the public interest, for scientific and historical
   research purposes or for statistical purposes in accordance with Article
   89(1) GDPR, having regard also to the implementation of the appropriate
   technical and organisational measures required by that Regulation to
   safeguard the rights and freedoms of data subjects ("**storage
   limitation**");
f) shall be processed in such a manner that, by applying appropriate technical
   or organisational measures, the appropriate security of the personal data
   is ensured, including protection against unauthorised or unlawful
   processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage ("integrity
   and confidentiality").

The controller is responsible for compliance with the above and must be able
to demonstrate that compliance ("accountability").

### 4.6. Use of body-temperature measurement on entry

The Data Controller may apply body-temperature measurement as a protective
measure extending uniformly to every person intending to enter the areas and
buildings it owns or uses.

Carrying out the temperature measurement does not require the subject to be
identified specifically for this processing purpose, and in no way involves
the recording, further storage or transmission of data. Thereafter the
services are used only by those persons whose entry may be permitted on the
basis of the temperature measurement.

**Rationale.** From the mere fact that a person's body temperature is elevated
it cannot be concluded that they are infected with a pathogen — for example
with some variant of the novel coronavirus. The Data Controller's staff
therefore draw no conclusion as to the person's state of health from the
temperature measurement taken on entry, while the Data Controller is entitled
to permit or to refuse entry.

The Data Controller thus stores no personal or health data, and takes a
decision solely as to whether to permit or refuse the entry of a person
wishing to enter its premises (on the ground that, according to the result of
the measurement, they carry a risk to other persons).

Where the person acting on the Data Controller's behalf refuses entry, further
handling of the situation is the Data Subject's task (consulting a doctor,
arranging sick leave and sick pay, informing their manager at work, and so
on); the Data Controller has no further task or responsibility in this regard.

      is no overriding legitimate ground for it;
   d) the personal data has been processed unlawfully;
   e) the personal data must be erased in order to comply with a legal
      obligation under Union or national law applicable to us; **or**
   f) the personal data was collected in connection with the provision of
      information society services.
2. Where the controller has disclosed the personal data and is obliged to
   erase it pursuant to paragraph 1, it shall take reasonable steps, including
   technical measures, having regard to the technology available and the cost

investigated immediately, entered in the register, and the necessary measures

- the time and manner of the complaint's arrival;

- a list of the papers and documents presented by the client;
- the Data Controller's position on the complaint, where immediate
  investigation of the complaint is possible;

- the place and time at which the record was drawn up;

  reference number.

Where the client does not agree with the handling of the complaint, the Data
Controller shall without delay draw up a record of the complaint, of its

to the client.

and under the same rules as a written complaint.

### Handling of written complaints

The Data Controller and its staff handle incoming complaints in the manner
prescribed by law. A record is drawn up of complaints, with a content similar
to that for an oral complaint. Unless a directly applicable legal act of the
European Union provides otherwise, the Data Controller is obliged to answer a
written complaint in writing and on the merits within 30 days of its receipt,
and to take steps to communicate the answer.

The Data Controller's head, or the administrator appointed by them, may hear
the complainant (the person making the report), or turn to external experts,
where the investigation of the complaint or report so requires.

The Data Controller endeavours to act quickly and to decide whether, under the
legislation in force, it will investigate the complaint, remedy it, or reject
it. It sends its decision to the person making the report with intelligible
and unambiguous reasons, responding to every problem raised.

Where a complaint is rejected, the Data Controller informs the client in
writing which authority's procedure, or the procedure of the conciliation
body, they may initiate with the complaint, according to its nature.

The Data Controller is obliged to retain the record drawn up of the complaint
and a copy of the answer for five years, and to present them to the
supervisory authorities on request.

A cookie is a small information package consisting of letters and numbers. The
web server automatically sends the cookie to the visitor's browser when the
site is visited for the first time. The visitor's computer or mobile device
stores the cookie for the period determined by the person who placed it.

On the next visit to the site, the browser sends the cookie back to the web
server. On the basis of the data sent, the web server can identify the
computer or mobile device that sent the cookie, and can link the cookies sent
by that device. The cookie supplies the web server with information about the
activity carried on between visits to the site. A web beacon is a small,
generally imperceptible image placed on a site. By placing web beacons, the
visitor's actions on the site can be followed and statistics compiled from the
data obtained.

Secret Hotel Kft. places cookies and web beacons on the site in order to
recognise a person who has visited the site before; to map the visitor's
field of interest; to improve the visitor's user experience and display
personalised advertisements to them; and in order to improve the security of
the site.

The operation of the Data Controller's site at https://www.storiesbudapest.com
is assisted by the following cookies:

- **Necessary cookies** — serve the basic operation of the site;
- **Functional cookies** — serve to remember user preferences;
- **Performance cookies** — assist the performance of the site and thereby
  help to provide a fuller user experience.

We inform our users that, under section 155(4) of Act C of 2003 on Electronic
Communications (the "Eht."), the use of the cookies operated by our site

notice appears at the bottom of the screen stating that the site uses cookies,
together with a link leading to this policy. The user may consent to the use
of cookies by pressing the "I allow" button.

Further information on the use of cookies is available at allaboutcookies.org,
which includes detailed guidance on erasing cookies from a computer. For

By using the site you accept the use of technical data and cookies as set out

The cookies used on the site do not store information suitable for
